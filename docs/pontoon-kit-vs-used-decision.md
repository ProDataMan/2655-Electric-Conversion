# Pontoon / Party Barge Decision: Used vs Bolt-Together Kits

**Context:** Goal is an economical electric party barge to pair with the 2655 conversion for an initial rental fleet of 2, scalable to 10+. Friends available to help with build. Preference noted for the “new” aspect of kits.

## Used Party Barges / Pontoons (Seattle / WA Market Signals)
- Typical listings (Craigslist, BoatTrader, etc., late 2025/early 2026):
  - 20–22 ft Sun Tracker / similar Party Barge: $23,000–$32,000 (with trailer, low-moderate hours motors).
  - 24–27 ft models / tri-toons: $25,000–$40,000+ depending on condition and power.
  - Older or larger patio-style: Lower entry ($12k–$30k) but more refurbishment needed.
- Pros:
  - Fastest path to water (existing deck, seating, railing, trailer often included).
  - Lower upfront capital.
  - Immediate rental testing possible after electric conversion.
- Cons for rental fleet:
  - Unknown structural history, potential corrosion/dents in tubes, worn wiring/upholstery.
  - Harder to optimize battery placement and high-voltage routing.
  - Inconsistent fleet appearance and maintenance profiles.
  - May require significant rework to meet commercial/rental standards and insurance.

## Bolt-Together Kits (e.g., Tinypontoonboats.com / BigAssPontoonBoats HDPE + Aluminum)
- Example pricing (frame + floats, freight extra):
  - Small recreational (6x11 to 8x15 ft): ~$6,000–$13,000.
  - Larger systems (15x15 tiki-style or scaled barge): $20,000–$50,000+ depending on size/capacity.
  - Massive commercial barges: $75,000–$115,000+ (overkill for typical party barge).
- Material: Foam-filled HDPE floats (unsinkable, corrosion-resistant) + aluminum frame (bolt-together, adjustable).
- Pros (especially with friends helping):
  - **New construction**: Clean, known materials, no hidden damage. Easier to design for electric from the start (dedicated battery compartments, weight distribution, wiring channels).
  - Customizable size, deck layout, transom for electric outboard (XL shaft options available).
  - Scalable process: Learn on first build, then repeat for fleet units with consistent quality.
  - Lower long-term maintenance; HDPE floats excellent for freshwater rental use.
  - “New” marketing appeal for rentals (“brand-new electric party barge”).
- Cons:
  - More labor (assembly, decking, seating, systems integration) — mitigated by friends helping.
  - Freight cost from East Coast (NH-based kits) significant for large systems.
  - Need to source/finish decking, railing, seating, helm separately.

## Recommendation
**Prioritize bolt-together kits for the primary party barge and future fleet expansion.**

Reasons:
1. Friends available to help → labor is an advantage, not a barrier. Builds skills and ownership.
2. Electric-first design is far cleaner on a new platform (battery weight low and centered, high-voltage safety, solar-ready if desired).
3. Consistency critical for a rental fleet of 10+: Same frames, same electric systems (e.g., Vision 180E, RAD 40, or ePropulsion X40), same maintenance procedures.
4. Long-term reliability and residual value higher with known-new construction.
5. Used barges still useful as a low-cost interim or secondary vessel for testing demand while the first kit build is completed.

**Suggested Path:**
- Short-term: Monitor local used market for a clean 20–24 ft Party Barge under $25k as a possible quick electric conversion (or keep as gas backup).
- Primary: Spec a 22–28 ft bolt-together kit sized for 10–15 passengers, with capacity for 40–80+ kWh batteries and a 40–135 kW electric outboard. Contact Tinypontoonboats / BigAss for custom sizing and freight quote to Washington.
- Standardize the electric powertrain across the fleet once the first successful build is validated.

**Next Actions:**
- Request formal kit quotes with specific dimensions (e.g., 24–26 ft platform, XL transom for electric outboard).
- Price local decking/seating materials and labor estimate with friends.
- Compare total landed + finished cost vs. a fully refurbished used barge + conversion.

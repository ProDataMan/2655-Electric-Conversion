# Fleet Scaling Considerations — From 2 to 10+ Electric Rental Vessels

**Starting Point**: Convert existing 1995 Bayliner 2655 + one electric party barge/pontoon. Operate as a two-boat rental fleet on Lake Union / Lake Washington. Expand if successful.

## Standardization Priorities

- **Common powertrain family**: Prefer 1-2 motor platforms across the fleet (e.g., Vision Marine or RAD or ePropulsion X-Series for pontoons; high-power for planing hulls). Reduces spare parts, training, and diagnostic tools.
- **Battery architecture**: Standardized voltage, BMS, and charging interfaces. Prefer LFP for cycle life and safety. Modular packs that can be swapped or paralleled.
- **Charging infrastructure**: Base station with multiple AC and DC fast-charge positions. Solar at dock where possible. Telemetry for SOC and health monitoring across vessels.
- **Controls & UX**: Consistent throttle/display or app experience so customers and operators need minimal training.
- **Maintenance**: Predictive via BMS data; local service partners; stock of critical spares (contactors, cooling components, props).

## Operational Considerations

- **Utilization**: Party barges for group events; 2655-style for mixed planing/fishing or premium runs. Scheduling software for bookings and charging windows.
- **Turnaround**: Fast charging or battery swap concepts for high utilization. Overnight shore charging as baseline.
- **Range management**: Clear customer guidance on expected range in different modes. Geofencing or alerts if needed.
- **Insurance & Compliance**: Electric recreational vessels; commercial rental policies; Washington State marine regulations; possible passenger vessel considerations as fleet grows.
- **Marketing**: Quiet, zero-emission, modern experience as differentiator on busy urban lakes.

## Economics

- Initial two vessels prove demand, pricing, and operating costs (energy, maintenance, downtime).
- Marginal cost of additional conversions should decrease with standardized kits and experience.
- Revenue from rentals + potential events or partnerships.
- Track kWh per rental hour, maintenance hours, and customer satisfaction to decide expansion pace.

## Expansion Path

1. Complete and validate 2655 + first pontoon.
2. Document conversion process, costs, and lessons into reusable playbooks.
3. Acquire next hulls (used pontoons preferred for speed and cost) while refining the design.
4. Scale charging and storage capacity at home base.
5. Consider mixed fleet (some pure displacement pontoons, some higher-performance planing hulls) to serve different customer segments.

## Risks & Mitigations

- **Motor lead times / availability**: Diversify slightly or secure early orders for preferred systems.
- **Battery cost & weight**: Continue monitoring LFP pricing and energy density improvements.
- **Regulatory**: Stay ahead of any electric vessel or commercial passenger rules.
- **Demand**: Start small; validate pricing and marketing before large capital commitment.

This repository will serve as the living design and operations knowledge base as the fleet grows.

# 1995 Bayliner 2655 Electric Conversion — Initial Design Plan

**Hull**: 1995 Bayliner 2655 Ciera, sterndrive planing hull, ~26-27 ft, dry weight ~5,175 lb, current condition: hole in 80-gallon gas tank, functional original motor (to be removed).

**Primary Mission**: Rental fleet vessel for Lake Union / Lake Washington mixed use — fast planing transit (520 bridge area to I-90 / Andrews Bay day parties) plus efficient displacement range for locks-to-Sound fishing and island hopping. Return planing legs after full-day use. Solar top-up while tied up.

## Design Objectives

1. Retain reasonable planing speed (~18-25 kt target loaded) for acceptable trip times.
2. Excellent efficiency at 4-8 kt for longer range and low energy cost.
3. Quiet, low-maintenance, zero-emission operation suitable for rental customers and urban lakes.
4. Battery capacity supporting full-day mixed use + return planing leg, with shore/fast charging for turnaround.
5. Scalable architecture that can be replicated across additional vessels.

## Powertrain Options (Ranked for this project)

### Preferred Near-Term Paths
- **Single high-power outboard** (Evoy Breeze 120+, Vision Marine 180E, or RAD 120 when available) mounted on transom after sterndrive removal. Simplest installation path; retains planing potential; easy service/swap for fleet.
- **Integrated inboard** (Bosch EDM 160 or Evoy inboard) adapted to existing or new sterndrive/shaft. Keeps traditional layout; high continuous power.
- **Dual mid-power** (twin RAD 40 or dual ePropulsion X40 / higher) for redundancy and maneuverability.

### Displacement-Focused Alternative
- Dual higher-power pods (ePropulsion POD 12/20 eSSA or future Helix-style) inspired by Swedish conversion. Excellent efficiency and docking, but planing may be limited or require very high power density units.

## Battery & Energy

- Target: **80-150+ kWh** usable LFP (or manufacturer HV packs). Prioritize cycle life (3,000+ cycles) and marine safety (IP67, BMS, thermal management).
- Placement: Engine room (freed by ICE removal) + under-deck or dedicated compartments. Weight distribution critical (Swedish conversion showed lighter overall).
- Charging: Shore power (AC) + CCS DC fast charge preferred for rental turnaround. Onboard charger integrated where possible.
- Solar: Roof/bimini flexible panels for top-up while anchored (Swedish forums discussed this; practical for day use).

## Estimated Energy Use (Order of Magnitude)

- Planing (20 kt class): High consumption, short range (tens of nm depending on pack and hull loading).
- Displacement (5-6 kt): Very efficient; Swedish 28.7 kWh example achieved ~50-60 nm calculated at 4 kt.
- Mixed day: Planing legs + hours of displacement/party use → size battery for the most demanding return leg + reserve.

## Structural & Installation Notes

- Remove sterndrive, bellows, and gas system completely (tank already compromised).
- Engine room becomes battery/electronics bay. Clean, dry, ventilated.
- Transom reinforcement if using high-power outboard.
- Dual independent drives (pods or twin outboards) improve low-speed handling for docking and rental users.
- Controls: Electronic throttle, display (NMEA/CAN), possible joystick or dual-throttle for maneuverability.
- Safety: Battery isolation, fire detection, emergency shutoff, marine-rated wiring and fusing.

## Rental-Specific Considerations

- Simple user interface (app or clear display for range/SOC).
- Fast charging infrastructure at home base.
- Predictive maintenance via BMS telemetry.
- Spare motor or modular components for minimal downtime.
- Insurance, certification, and local regulations for electric recreational vessels.
